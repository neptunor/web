Drop-stitch construction uses thousands of polyester threads connecting the top and bottom PVC layers of a panel. When inflated to high working pressure, these threads hold the faces at a fixed distance, creating a flat, rigid plate that behaves like a solid floor inside an otherwise flexible boat.

This is what turns a folded dinghy into a platform you can stand on, plane across a bay or load gear into — and it rolls back up when the day is done.

## Where it appears in our boats

Drop-stitch panels form the air deck floors of our tenders, the seat bases and footrests in open RIBs, and optional bow platforms where a stiff, flat surface earns its space. Panels are welded into the hull line so they cannot shift under load.

## Specs you can quote

Neptunor panels use high-tenacity drop-stitch fabric with a reinforced PVC laminate, rated for high working pressure. Every batch is validated with air-retention holds before shipment, alongside the tube chambers it ships with.
