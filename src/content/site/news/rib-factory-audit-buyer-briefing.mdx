A factory audit should answer more than “does this supplier have a workshop?” For RIB and inflatable-boat buyers, the useful question is whether the supplier can repeatedly build the agreed configuration, prove what happened to each production batch, and support the boat after delivery.

This briefing turns the source material into a practical review sequence. It is not a certification of any supplier, and buyers should verify evidence against the exact model, configuration and destination market.

## 1. Confirm the manufacturing identity

Start with the legal entity, factory address, trading name and brand relationship. The name on the quotation, purchase order, product identification, compliance documents and warranty terms should be understandable and consistent.

During a visit or live video review, ask to see the actual RIB and inflatable-boat workflow: incoming materials, hull or tube production, assembly, inspection, packing and finished-goods storage. Also ask which processes are performed in-house and which are subcontracted.

## 2. Follow one boat through the quality gates

A useful audit does not stop at a presentation. Select one representative model and follow its route through:

- material identification and incoming inspection;
- hull, tube, bonding or welding operations;
- dimensional and configuration checks;
- inflation, pressure-hold or leak testing where applicable;
- final inspection, packing and shipment release.

Ask for blank inspection forms as well as completed, redacted examples. The purpose is to understand the control method, acceptance criteria, responsible role and action taken when a result fails.

## 3. Test traceability with a sample record

Request a demonstration using a sample serial number or production reference. A controlled system should be able to connect the boat to the approved model revision, key material batch, production date, inspection results and shipment record, subject to confidentiality and customer-data limits.

The record does not need to expose another customer's commercial information. It should show that the supplier can retrieve the relevant evidence without relying on memory or disconnected spreadsheets.

## 4. Check documents against the actual configuration

For an EU-bound recreational RIB, review the document structure rather than accepting a generic “CE certificate” label. Confirm how the model, design category, hull identification, builder's plate, owner manual, EU Declaration of Conformity and technical-file references are controlled.

The same discipline applies to material documents: a technical data sheet, certificate of analysis or batch record is useful only when it can be matched to the material and configuration used for the order. See the <a href="/resources/rib-compliance-documentation">RIB compliance documentation guide</a> for a detailed review path.

## 5. Verify capacity and lead-time logic

Do not ask only for an annual capacity number. Ask what the number means: product mix, working shifts, bottleneck process, material availability, inspection capacity, packing method and current production loading.

A credible lead-time discussion should identify the start point—such as specification freeze, approved sample, confirmed purchase order or deposit—and the events that can change the schedule. Sample timing and bulk-production timing should be quoted separately.

## 6. Ask how problems are closed

Review the non-conforming-product process, rework approval, corrective action and customer complaint handling. Ask who decides whether a failed boat is repaired, reworked, re-inspected or rejected, and how the decision is recorded.

Also ask about spare parts, replacement components, repair instructions and warranty claim evidence. After-sales readiness is part of supply reliability, especially for rental, resort, rescue and multi-site fleet programs.

## A practical next step

Prepare a model-specific audit list before the visit, then request the exact evidence needed for your target market.
