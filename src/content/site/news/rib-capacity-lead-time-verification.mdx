When a RIB supplier quotes capacity or lead time, the important question is not the biggest number in a brochure. It is whether the factory can reserve the required process steps, materials, inspection time and shipping window for the buyer's exact configuration.

Capacity and schedule discussions should therefore be specific to the model, material, customization level, quantity and destination—not treated as universal promises.

## Separate the production stages

Ask the supplier to distinguish at least these milestones:

1. requirement and configuration review;
2. artwork, GA or engineering approval;
3. sample or pilot build;
4. sample inspection and specification freeze;
5. material preparation and production;
6. in-process and final inspection;
7. packing, container loading and shipping-document release.

A “30-day lead time” is meaningful only when the start event and included activities are written down. Sample development should normally be quoted separately from repeat production, especially when the order includes new tooling, a new layout or custom branding.

## Identify the bottleneck

For inflatable boats and RIBs, the limiting step may be tube welding or bonding, hull fabrication, console and hardware supply, engine coordination, inspection, packing or container availability. Ask how the supplier plans around the bottleneck during peak ordering periods.

Useful evidence can include a redacted production schedule, work-in-progress review, material availability check, staffing plan and inspection-capacity explanation. The goal is not to demand another customer's confidential order data; it is to test whether the quoted schedule has an operational basis.

## Define what changes the date

The quotation should state the events that can move the schedule: delayed artwork approval, specification changes, late payment, substituted components, certification review, customer inspection, port congestion or a change in packing requirements. A documented change-control process is more useful than an unconditional promise that ignores these variables.

For multi-container or fleet orders, request a shipment plan showing lot split, inspection point, packing method and expected release sequence. Use the <a href="/resources/rib-export-packing">export packing and loading guide</a> to align the shipping assumptions before production begins.

## Connect capacity to quality

More output is not automatically better if inspection and traceability do not scale with it. Ask how each production lot is identified, which checks are completed before release, how failed units are contained and how records are retained for after-sales review.

A reliable factory should be able to explain how the approved sample, specification, inspection checklist and shipment record remain connected when volume increases. Buyers can review the <a href="/resources/rib-traceability-evidence">traceability evidence guide</a> and <a href="/factory-audit-checklist">factory audit checklist</a> before a site review.

## Put the commitment in the RFQ

State the model or platform, tube material, hull type, quantity, customization scope, target market, required documents, inspection plan, packing method and target shipment window. This gives the factory enough context to return a project-specific schedule rather than a generic marketing estimate.
